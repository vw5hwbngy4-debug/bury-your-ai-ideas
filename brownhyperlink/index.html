Brown Hyperlinks / Burylinks

Blue links open pages.
Brown links hide worlds.

A brown hyperlink is a discoverable hyperlink: it does not open a destination directly, but turns the destination into a buried artifact that must be recovered before access.

create a brown hyperlink

Definition

Brown Hyperlinks, also called Burylinks, are a web interaction primitive for recoverable discovery. A blue hyperlink delivers a page. A brown hyperlink creates a field-state: the user must dig, reveal, or recover the artifact before the linked content becomes visible.

Blue hyperlink

direct source page

Function: direct navigation.

Grammar: click → open → consume.

State: stable, canonical, surfaced, already addressable.

Brown hyperlink

recovered field archive

Function: delayed discovery.

Grammar: click → enter field → dig → reveal.

State: hidden, disputed, spoiler-safe, unfinished, ritualized, recoverable.

Why brown?

The early web normalized blue links through browser convention. Brown links use a different semantic color: soil, depth, buried memory, stubs, spoilers, uncertain sources, field archives, and recoverable media.

Brown is not a replacement for blue. It is a second category.

The Brownweb

The Brownweb is the recoverable layer of the existing web. It is not a separate internet. It is a depth layer where links become artifacts before they become destinations.

The old web was something you surfed. The Brownweb is something you dig.

Natural uses

  • spoilers and soft reveals
  • leaks and must-be-verified sources
  • ARGs and hidden tracks
  • easter eggs and secret communities
  • game unlocks and buried patch notes
  • albums, fan lore, recovered archives

Not just hidden links

Ordinary hidden links are accidental, deceptive, decorative, or puzzle-specific. A burylink is a generalized interaction grammar: a deliberate link state for positive discovery friction.

It makes “not-yet-opened” meaningful.

The web had surfing.
It never had digging.

Canonical claim

Brown Hyperlinks establish a semantic distinction between direct access and recoverable access. They reframe hyperlinking from surface navigation to field discovery: page becomes artifact, click becomes excavation, link becomes memory.
